# Regional championship allocation — planning policy

Status: DRAFT; regions below are candidates, not verified eligible-club counts or final competition boundaries.

## Core rule
A regional championship covers a contiguous cluster of counties containing enough **verified eligible Top 100 member clubs** to support a viable singles and pairs draw, while minimising travel for early rounds.

## Proposed initial county clusters
- Surrey: Surrey
- Dorset & Hampshire: Dorset, Hampshire
- Berkshire & Oxfordshire: Berkshire, Oxfordshire, Buckinghamshire
- Sussex & Kent: East Sussex, West Sussex, Kent
- Midlands: Warwickshire, West Midlands, Worcestershire, Staffordshire
- East Midlands: Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire, Leicestershire, Lincolnshire
- Yorkshire: North, West, South and East Yorkshire; split into zones if density allows
- North West: Cheshire, Lancashire, Merseyside
- South West: Somerset, Devon, Cornwall; may require sub-zones to avoid long drives
- East of England: Norfolk, Suffolk, Essex, Cambridgeshire, Hertfordshire; may require splitting

## Allocation algorithm specification
1. Select **one canonical ranking**, edition and course/club mapping (proposed: Top 100 Golf Courses, England 2026); count *clubs* once even if multiple courses qualify.
2. Vet membership eligibility, operational status, location and each club's canonical county.
3. Set a minimum club count (provisional planning threshold: 5 eligible clubs per region) **and** minimum verified entrants (provisional: 16 singles, 8 pairs). These are independent conditions and must be tested before final boundaries.
4. Start with compact county clusters; merge with **adjacent** counties only if under threshold. Consider realistic driving times between member clubs, not just geographic adjacency. Limit typical early-round journeys (provisional goal: 60–90 minutes one way); allow exceptions in sparse regions only with disclosure.
5. Where regions are too large, divide into subregions/pods for the first rounds, then host a regional final. Avoid cross-country early-round fixtures.
6. Assign entrants by their verified *home club*, not residential postcode. Document treatment of border clubs and appeals.
7. Use independent singles and pairs competition records, regional qualification quotas and venue capacity. Don't promise national-final places until quotas are set.
8. Never publish club counts, geographic coverage or registration status based on unverified course lists.
